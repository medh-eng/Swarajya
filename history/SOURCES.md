# Sources

Every `Refs:` ID in `CHRONOLOGY.md` must appear in this file (the checker enforces it).
Three groups: **P** primary (period) sources, **S** modern scholarship, **W** web references
actually read while building the chronology (23 Nov 2026). Web references are where *we* read a
fact; the History notes in the game should cite the P and S works those pages rest on.

**Quality rule:** a fact resting only on a W source marked *weak* is `VERIFY` and cannot be used in
the game. Encyclopedias (Wikipedia, Britannica) are acceptable for dates and outcomes, but each
chapter's History note must also name the primary source the encyclopedia cites.

## P · Primary and near-contemporary sources

| ID | Source | What it is | Use with care because |
|---|---|---|---|
| P-SABHASAD | Krishnaji Anant Sabhasad, *Shri Shiva Prabhuche Charitra* (Sabhasad Bakhar), 1697 | Marathi chronicle written at Jinji for Rajaram; the most trusted bakhar | Written 17 years after Shivaji's death; partisan; large numbers |
| P-JEDHE | *Jedhe Shakavali* and *Jedhe Kareena* | Chronology kept by the Jedhe deshmukh family of Kari | Terse; some dates disputed (e.g. birth year) |
| P-SHIVBHARAT | Kavindra Paramananda, *Shivabharata* (Sanskrit) | Epic by Shivaji's court poet, contemporary; breaks off around 1661 | Poetic, presents Shivaji as divine; dates and names are useful, battle scenes are not |
| P-EFR | English East India Company factory records (Surat, Rajapur, Bombay) | Letters, e.g. Henry Revington 10 Dec 1659; Narayan Shenvi from Raigad 4 Apr 1674; Surat 1664 | Outsiders' view; rumour mixed with report |
| P-DILKASHA | Bhimsen Saxena (Burhanpuri), *Nuskha-i-Dilkasha* | Persian memoir of a Mughal officer; eyewitness at Vani 1670 | Mughal side |
| P-PERSIAN | Mughal court histories (e.g. *Maasir-i-Alamgiri*) and Bijapur histories (e.g. *Tarikh-i-Ali*) | The adversaries' accounts | Hostile to Shivaji; call him a rebel |
| P-RAJASTHANI | Letters of the Amber (Jaipur) court, 1665–66 | Jai Singh's and Ram Singh's agents on Purandar and Agra | Amber's interest; translations vary |
| P-CHITNIS | Chitnis Bakhar | Later Maratha chronicle (early 19th c.) | Late; embellished; use only where it differs interestingly (e.g. Tuljapur) |
| P-POWADA | Powadas (ballads): *Afzal Khan Vadh* (Agrindas), Tanaji's powada (Tulsidas) | Marathi heroic ballads | TRADITION only; the game's narrator draws on their style, not their facts |

## Standard reference (Pon's decision, 23 Nov 2026)

**S-PURANDARE** — Babasaheb (B. M.) Purandare, *Raja Shivachhatrapati* (राजा शिवछत्रपती), Purandare
Prakashan, Pune. Marathi, two volumes (first published 1950s–60s; 19th ed. 2014, about 934 pp.);
English translation, two volumes (2014; current ed. 2021, about 895 pp.).
**Pon's copy: the English edition, latest printing** (24 Nov 2026). Cite it as `S-PURANDARE` with volume and chapter.
The most widely read life of Shivaji Maharaj, written from a lifetime's study of the documents,
in a narrative style. It is the project's **standard reference**: chapters, people and the order of
events are checked against it first.

How it is used (also in CLAUDE.md):
1. Each chronology entry is checked against it (`history/PURANDARE-CHECK.md`) and cites it by chapter.
2. Where it agrees with the period sources (P-*) and Mehendale/Sarkar: HISTORY.
3. Where it adds detail the period sources do not have: shown with Purandare named as the source;
   marked TRADITION if he presents it as tradition.
4. Where it differs from a period source, or the point is publicly disputed: both versions go into
   `CONTESTED.md` and **Pon decides** what the game shows. Known case: the role of Dadoji Kondadeo
   (CONTESTED #6), which was at the centre of the 2015 public controversy about the book. Pon decided
   (D-15) to follow the book and show Dadoji as teacher, with the dispute noted.
5. We do not copy its text into the game. Facts and order of events only, in our own words.

## S · Modern scholarship (for the History notes)

| ID | Work | Note |
|---|---|---|
| S-PURANDARE | B. M. (Babasaheb) Purandare, *Raja Shivachhatrapati* (Marathi; English trans. 2014) | **Standard reference** (see above) |
| S-SARKAR | Jadunath Sarkar, *Shivaji and His Times* (1919, later eds.) | Long the standard English work; based heavily on Persian and English records; later Marathi scholarship corrected several points |
| S-MEHENDALE | Gajanan Bhaskar Mehendale, *Shivaji: His Life and Times* (English ed. 2011) | Detailed, source-critical; the best single check for dates |
| S-TAKAKHAV | N. S. Takakhav and K. A. Keluskar, *The Life of Shivaji Maharaj* (1921; online ed. 2017) | Older Maratha-side narrative; useful detail, check against Mehendale |
| S-GORDON | Stewart Gordon, *The Marathas 1600–1818* (Cambridge, 1993) | Balanced academic overview |
| S-SARDESAI | G. S. Sardesai, *New History of the Marathas* | Classic Marathi-school history |
| S-APTE | B. K. Apte, *A History of the Maratha Navy and Merchantships* | Navy, Khanderi |
| S-LAINE-SB | J. W. Laine with S. S. Bahulkar, *The Epic of Shivaji: Kavindra Paramananda's Sivabharata* (2001) | Translation of P-SHIVBHARAT |
| S-DUFF | James Grant Duff, *A History of the Mahrattas* (1826) | Early British history; dated |
| S-KINCAID | Dennis Kincaid, *The Grand Rebel* / C. A. Kincaid & Parasnis, *A History of the Maratha People* | Readable, dated |

**Not used as a source of facts:** James Laine, *Shivaji: Hindu King in Islamic India* (2003). The book is the subject of a long controversy in Maharashtra; its claims about Shivaji's parentage are rejected by Maratha historians and are not used in any form in this game. (It may be cited only as historiography if ever needed, which we do not expect.)
